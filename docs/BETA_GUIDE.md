# Meditaur — Beta Tester Guide

Thank you for helping test Meditaur before it opens to everyone.

This guide tells you what to do, in what order, and exactly how to tell us what
you find. It is written for a first-time user, not for a developer — you do not
need to know anything technical to take part, and there are no wrong answers.

> **This beta:** **https://meditaur-web.vercel.app/** — nothing to install, and
> no account needed. Everything you do is saved in the browser you use, on your
> own device; an account, if you make one, carries your settings and nothing else.
> Send your notes to
> whoever invited you; see
> [What to write down, and how](#7-what-to-write-down-and-how).

## 1. What we are asking of you

**Please:**

- Use Meditaur the way you would actually use it, at least twice, on a real session.
- Try the rough edges on purpose. If something looks odd, poke at it.
- Tell us when something is confusing, even if nothing is broken. Confusion is a
  result. "I did not know what to press" is exactly the kind of report we want.
- Tell us what you expected to happen, not just what happened.

**Please do not:**

- Try to break it by deleting your browser data or clearing cookies mid-test.
  You will lose your work, and that is not something we need to know about.
- Enter anything sensitive into your intention lines. See
  [Your privacy](#9-your-privacy).
- Spend more than about 45 minutes in total unless you are enjoying it. Progress
  reports matter more than volume.

**Time we are asking for:** roughly 30–45 minutes for the guided plan, plus
whatever real sessions you want to do.

## 2. Getting set up

Before you start, please check these off:

| | Check |
| --- | --- |
| ☐ | **A computer, tablet or phone** with a reasonably up-to-date browser (Chrome, Edge, Safari or Firefox). |
| ☐ | **Headphones.** The binaural tones need them. Without headphones you are testing a different experience. |
| ☐ | **Sound on**, and your device volume turned up to something comfortable. |
| ☐ | **A quiet room** and a few minutes where you will not be interrupted. |
| ☐ | **The web address**: **https://meditaur-web.vercel.app/** |
| ☐ | **A charger**, if you are on a phone and planning a long session. |
| ☐ | **Somewhere to jot notes** as you go — a notepad, or a note on your phone. |

**Optional but helpful: install it as an app.** In most browsers, the browser's
menu offers something like *Install* or *Add to Home Screen*. Meditaur works the
same either way, but installing it gives a fuller-screen, more app-like feel, and
it is worth telling us how that went if you try it.

**Important:** use the same browser for the whole test. Meditaur keeps your
material in the browser you are using, so a different browser — or a private
window — will look like a fresh, empty start.

## 3. Your first 20 minutes

Follow this in order. Do not skip ahead; the point is to see it the way a new
person would.

1. **Open https://meditaur-web.vercel.app/.** You should land on a welcome screen
   with the name `Meditaur`, and the buttons `Start session` and `Open planner`.
   - Write down what you think each of the three buttons (`Start session`,
     `Open planner`, `Account`) does, before you press any of them. This is
     genuinely useful to us.
2. **Put your headphones on.**
3. **Press `Start session`.** You should arrive at a run screen showing your
   intentions for the first block, with a `Start` button. Nothing is playing yet.
   - Take a moment. Is it obvious what to press? How do you know nothing is
     playing yet?
4. **Press `Start`.** The countdown should begin and a low tone should fade in.
   - Note how long it took for sound to appear, and whether the tone was
     comfortable.
5. **Let one block finish.** When the timer reaches zero, an alarm should sound
   and the next block should start on its own.
   - Did the alarm catch you by surprise? Was it too loud or too quiet?
6. **Press the space bar twice.** It should pause, then resume.
7. **Press the right arrow key.** It should move to the next stage and hold there,
   waiting for `Start` (pressing it twice quickly moves to the next meditation).
8. **Press Escape.** It should return you to the planner.
9. **Open `Library`** from the top navigation. Press each of the eight section
   tabs: `Meditations`, `Symbols`, `Database`, `Archive`, `Audio files`,
   `Presets`, `Plans`, `History`.
   - Do not try to understand everything. Just notice whether you can figure out
     what each section is for.
10. **Open `History`.** Your completed session should be there, with the plan's name
    and a duration.
11. **Open `Settings`** and change `Text size` to `XL`. Everything should grow.
    Change it back if you prefer.
12. **Go back to `Plan` and look at the row of focus tiles at the top**, under
    `Chakras`, `Point` and `Custom`. **Do not press one yet** — just tell us
    whether you could tell, from looking, that pressing one would immediately
    start a session.

Now write down your first impressions, before you learn anything else. First
impressions from a fresh pair of eyes are the most valuable thing you can give
us.

## 4. The guided test plan

Work through these at your own pace. Each one is a short task with a specific
question attached. Skipping one is fine — say which you skipped, and why.

### Task 1 — A full session, start to finish

**Do:** Build a short plan of your own and run it to the end.

1. On `Plan`, press `New plan`.
2. Press `Add meditation block`, type a few letters of a meditation's name in the
   text bar and pick it.
3. Set the stage lengths to something short — one or two minutes each — with the
   `Minutes` and `Seconds` wheels on the stage rows in the block's editor. Press
   `Edit` on a card to open it, and `Done` when you are finished.
4. Add another block the same way if you want a longer session.
5. Press `Start session`.

**Watch for:** Did the plan behave the way you expected? When the session finished,
did you know it had finished? Did you know what to do next?

### Task 2 — Symbol choices

**Do:** On the same plan, open a block's editor — `Edit` on its card — and use its
`Symbol` section to try `Rotate next`, `All symbols`, and one symbol by name. Then
run a block with each.

**Watch for:** Was it clear what each option would do *before* you chose it? Did
the run screen show what you expected?

### Task 3 — Meditation and symbol material

**Do:** In `Library` → `Meditations`, open a meditation — that page only shows,
so press `Edit` and finish in the Database (`Database` → `Karuna`, where a row is
is a meditation, a symbol, or a pair of the two and the intentions are written inside
it). Then open that meditation's blocks in a plan and sit through one.

**Watch for:** How many steps did it take to get from "I want this on screen
during my session" to actually seeing it? Was any step a surprise? On the run
screen the symbol in play gets a panel of its own — its picture and its name — that swaps as you scroll into the next group, while the intentions are one table with a hairline between its rows. Did that
help you follow along, or did it read as clutter? And a symbol with no
picture of its own shows only its name — was that enough to tell
the symbols apart while you were reading?

### Task 4 — Your own words

**Do:** In `Database` (the tab beside `Library`) → `Karuna`, edit one of the
starter lines
inside a row — that is an intention — and replace it with something of your own
that you are comfortable having in a test. Add a brand-new line with `＋ Add`.
Then press `Save`.

**Watch for:** Was it obvious that a row is a chakra, a symbol, or a pair, and
that the lines inside it belong to that pair? Did the line appear where you
expected during a session? And the Database is the one screen that **throws away
unsaved edits** when you leave it — did the `Unsaved changes` label and the
question on the way out tell you that clearly enough?

### Task 5 — Sound of your own

**Do:** In `Library` → `Audio files`, add a short audio file from your device as
an alarm. Then set it in that block's editor, under `Sound` → `Alarm`. Run that
block.

**Watch for:** Did the file upload straightforwardly? Did the block wait for your
alarm to finish before moving on? (A longer alarm file should hold the block
open longer.)

### Task 6 — Binaural beats

**Do:** Open `Library` → `Meditations` → any meditation → `Edit` →
`Open binaural config`. Press `Try` to hear the sound. Press `Duplicate`. Change
something — add a tone, move an EQ slider. Press `Try` again. Then press
`Revert`, and finally `Save`.

Then adjust a preset's sound. The easiest way is `Library` → `Presets` → `Edit`
on a preset: the tones, fades and EQ are on that page, with `Save`. The tuner at
`/tuner` has the same controls and plays while you change them.

**Watch for:** Was it obvious which changes were saved and which were not? Did
you feel safe experimenting? Did the `Try` preview behave as you expected?

### Task 7 — Turning things off on purpose

**Do:** On `Plan`, turn the `Binaural beats` master switch off and run a block.
Turn it back on. Then, in the Library, open a meditation and turn *its*
`Binaural beats` switch off, and run that meditation's block in a plan.

**Watch for:** Could you tell which switch was doing what? This is the part of
Meditaur people find hardest, and we would like to know how it felt to you.

### Task 8 — Backup and restore

**Do:** Press `Download catalog` in the Library and keep the file. Make one
visible change (add a meditation named `Beta test`). Then press `Restore
catalog` and choose the file you downloaded. Check whether `Beta test` is still
there, and whether anything looks duplicated.

**Watch for:** Was it clear what the file was and where it went? Would you have
known to do this *before* something going wrong?

### Task 9 — Spoken intentions

**Do:** Turn on `Speak intentions (TTS)` in `Settings`. Leave the run screen and
come back, then start a session on a block that has two or three intentions.

**Watch for:** Did the reading start on its own? Was the pace and volume usable?
Did it get cut off?

### Task 10 — Something goes wrong, on purpose

**Do:** Start a session, then refresh the page.

**Watch for:** Did the app tell you what had happened? What did you expect to
happen?

## 5. What we most want to hear about

We are testing the *experience*, not just whether buttons respond. In rough
order of usefulness to us:

1. **Where you got stuck.** Anything that made you stop and think "what now?".
2. **Where the words did not match your expectations.** Did "intention",
   "symbol", "block", "cycle" or "meditation" mean what you assumed they meant?
3. **The binaural switches.** Did the plan-wide switch, and the stage's own
   `Binaural` switch, make sense?
4. **Saving, and the fear of losing work.** Did you feel confident your work was
   safe? Did you know your material lives in this browser until you download a
   catalog file?
5. **The feel of the run screen.** Too sparse? Too busy? Too small or large? Could
   you read it from where you sit?
6. **Sound quality and volume.** Was the tone comfortable? Was the alarm right?
   Could you hear your intentions being read?
7. **Doing it with your eyes closed, or from across the room.** Could you find
   what you needed without hunting?
8. **Your own practice.** Did Meditaur fit around it, or did it get in the way?
   What was missing?

## 6. Known rough edges — you do not need to report these

These are already on our list. Please do not spend your time on them, and do not
let them spoil your test. If one of them bothers you more than you expected,
that *is* worth telling us.

- **`Erase this device's data` on the Account screen deletes everything, with no
  undo and no export first.** It is meant for exactly that — it re-seeds the
  starter content and signs you out — but `Download catalog` is the only way back,
  so press that first if you are testing on material you care about.
- **Some labels read like the insides of the app rather than English.** The run
  screen labels the current block as `Root Chakra · cycle 1` (the meditation's
  name, a middle dot, then the cycle). This is cosmetic and known.
- **You can ignore the `Account` button.** The welcome screen has one and you
  never need it: press `Continue locally` and everything works. If you would
  rather have your testing attached to an account you can create one there — an
  account carries your `Settings` between the devices you sign in on, while your
  material (meditations, symbols, intentions, plans, sounds) stays in this
  browser. Changing a setting while signed in does need a connection; testing
  without an account works offline exactly as before.
- **Clearing a custom field's box deletes that value** rather than saving an empty
  one. This is intended, but it surprises people.
- **Restoring the same catalog file twice can duplicate some items.** Restore into
  a fresh app when you can.
- **History under-counts long sessions.** A completed session is recorded as one pass
  through the plan's blocks, so a plan repeated three times appears with a shorter
  duration than it actually ran. The number of sessions is right; the duration is not.
- **There is no undo anywhere**, and no trash can. `Download catalog` is the
  safety net — and it matters more now: **deleting cascades**. Removing a chakra,
  symbol, preset, row or line also removes or clears whatever pointed at it, so a
  plan that used it changes too. The press tells you what it will take
  (`This also removes 2 plan blocks and 1 intention.`, `1 place that pointed at it
  is cleared.`) — read that line before the second press. **Archiving is the
  gentler half**: it hides the item and everything that depends on it — its rows,
  their lines, and the plan blocks that used it — and `Restore`, on the Archive
  page, brings all of it back exactly as it was.
- **A session cannot be resumed after a reload.** It starts again from the beginning.
- **`Delete plan` hides itself when you only have one plan**, with no explanation,
  and refuses the delete (`Keep at least one plan`). The same applies to your last
  preset (`Keep at least one preset`) — those two are the only deletes that refuse.
  (The last table view used to refuse too; a plan stores its own display now.)
- **Every delete arms first** — press once to fill it and ask, press again to do
  it. The only remove that does not is `Remove tone` in the tuner and in a focus
  point's binaural page, where the row is an unsaved draft and `Revert` is the
  undo.
- **Empty Library sections show blank space.** There is no "you have nothing
  here yet" guidance while a list is empty, except in History.
- **Starting a session from a focus tile in the planner is instant**, with no
  confirmation, and creates a plan called `Focus session` which then appears in your
  plan list.
- **The tuner saves as you go**, with no undo and no Save button. A preset's own
  editor does not: it has a `Save`, so that is the safer place to work.
- **The tuner has no entry in the navigation bar.** You reach it by its own web
  address (`/tuner`), and its controls also live inside a preset's editor
  (`Library` → `Presets` → `Edit`). If you had trouble finding it, that is not
  you.
- **When the tuner opens without being told which sound to use, it shows your
  first preset** rather than the one you might have expected.

## 7. What to write down, and how

**Write things down as you go**, not at the end. Keep a note on your phone or a
piece of paper beside you, and add a line whenever something surprises you —
including when something goes *well*.

**Send your notes to whoever invited you to this beta.** There is no form and no
feedback tool: one person is reading everything by hand, which is why a short and
specific message is more useful than a long and tidy one.

You do not need to write neatly or at length. Short and specific beats long and
vague. A one-line message like *"I couldn't tell if my changes were saved"* is a
genuinely useful report.

### The template

Copy this and fill in as much as you can. Leave anything blank that does not
apply.

```
What I was doing:
  (for example: "Adding an intention to the Heart Chakra")

What I expected to happen:
  (for example: "It would appear under that meditation")

What actually happened:
  (for example: "Nothing appeared, and no message showed up")

How to make it happen again:
  1.
  2.
  3.

Where I was:
  (the screen name, for example: Library → Meditations → Heart Chakra)

How much it got in my way:
  Blocker / Annoying / Minor / Just an idea

What I was using:
  Device (phone / tablet / computer):
  Browser (Chrome / Safari / Edge / Firefox):
  Headphones (yes / no):

Anything else:
  (a screenshot or short recording is very welcome — long-press or right-click
   and "Save image", or just screenshot the whole screen)
```

If screenshots or a template feel like too much, just write an e-mail. Anything
is better than nothing.

### How much it got in my way

Use one of these four words for every report. It helps us sort them.

| Word | Use it when |
| --- | --- |
| **Blocker** | You could not carry on with what you were doing. |
| **Annoying** | You got there eventually, but it bothered you. |
| **Minor** | You noticed it, and got on with your session. |
| **Just an idea** | Nothing is wrong; you think it could be better. |

## 8. What a good report looks like

**Useful:**

> **Annoying** — I was building a plan and pressed `Add meditation block` twice by
> accident, so now there are two blocks I did not want. I couldn't see any way to undo it,
> only `Remove` on each card, which felt a bit scary because I wasn't sure if it
> would take something else with it. On a computer, Chrome, with headphones.

Why it is useful: it says what you were doing, what you felt, which screen you
were on, and how much it mattered. There is no jargon, and it does not try to
guess what the fix should be.

**Not very useful:**

> The planner is broken.

Why it is not useful: we cannot tell what to look at, or how much it mattered.

**Also useful, and often skipped:**

> **Just an idea** — Everything worked. I just wanted to say that I could not tell
> whether my intentions had saved, because nothing acknowledged it.

Nothing was broken. We still want to know.

## 9. Your privacy

Worth knowing before you start writing your own material:

- **Your material stays in your browser.** During this beta, nothing you type,
  record or upload is sent to us. An account is optional and stores one thing:
  your `Settings` (volumes, text size, and the rest), so they follow you between
  the devices you sign in on. No analytics, and no server holding your content.
  **One exception is on its way:** the app can now send a crashed screen's message
  and its stack to the project so it can be fixed, and the table that receives them
  has been built — nothing sends one yet, and this guide will say so plainly on the
  day something does.
- **What that means practically:** your meditations, symbols, intentions, plans
  and uploaded sounds live only on the device and browser you are using — with or
  without an account. If you clear that browser's data, they are gone. If you open
  a different browser, you will see a fresh start; your settings are the one thing
  that comes with you.
- **Keep your catalog file to yourself.** `Download catalog` produces one file
  containing everything, including your written intentions and any audio you
  uploaded. It is a good backup, and a bad thing to post publicly. If you need to
  send us a copy to reproduce something, send only that file, deliberately, and
  we will say so explicitly if we ever need it.
- **Do not put anything sensitive into the app during the beta** — health details,
  names of real people, or anything you would not want in a screenshot. The
  starter content contains a real practitioner's example lines, and some of them
  contain blank markers like `<>` where a detail belongs. Replace them with
  something of your own, or ignore them.
- **Screenshots you send us** may show your intentions on screen. Have a look
  before you send one, and crop if you would rather not share the words.
- **Speech is your device's speech.** When `Speak intentions (TTS)` is on,
  Meditaur hands the words to your own device to read aloud. Which voice is used,
  and anything the device does with that, is governed by your device's settings,
  not by Meditaur.

If you are not comfortable with any of that, tell us, and we will find another
way for you to take part.

## 10. What happens next

1. **You test**, at your own pace, for as long as the beta is open — we will tell
   you when it closes.
2. **You send reports**, in whatever form is easiest for you.
3. **We read everything.** Every report is read. We may come back to you with a
   question, and we will tell you if a change was made because of something you
   found.
4. **We fix what blocks people**, first, then the annoyances, then the
   improvements — which is why the "how much it got in my way" word matters.
5. **We tell you what changed** when the next version goes out, and we will ask
   you to try the specific things we changed.

If you have one hour to give and want to spend it where it counts most: do
[Task 1](#task-1--a-full-session-start-to-finish),
[Task 3](#task-3--meditation-and-symbol-material) and
[Task 7](#task-7--turning-things-off-on-purpose) properly, and send us what
happened. Those three teach us the most.
